The Lab / Room Study

Small Flat, Big Green

Floreamus Una

A small space doesn't mean fewer plants. It means choosing them carefully. This room study shows how to make a compact living room feel calmer and more open, step by step.

This is an example room, not a real client project. It's a template you can adapt to your own space. Designer spotlights are coming soon.

The brief

Picture a typical city flat: a living room of around 12 square metres, one window, a sofa, a TV and not much floor space. The goal is to make it feel calmer and more open, with plants that cope with the light it actually gets.

Step 1: Map the light

Before buying anything, spend a day noticing where the light falls. Most rooms have three zones:

  • Bright: within a metre or so of the window.
  • Medium: two to three metres back, where you can still read comfortably in daylight.
  • Low: corners and spots far from the window.

Matching plants to these zones matters more than anything else you do.

Step 2: One statement plant

In a small room, one tall plant does more than five small ones. A Rubber Plant or Fiddle Leaf Fig in the bright zone draws the eye upward and makes the ceiling feel higher. If you have pets, a Parlour Palm gives a similar effect and is non-toxic.

Step 3: Go vertical

When floor space is short, use the walls. A shelf or two above the sofa holds trailing plants like Heartleaf Philodendron or the pet-safe Spider Plant, letting their stems soften hard edges. Our hanging shelf project is ideal for the window itself.

Step 4: Fill the low-light corners

Dim corners are where many plants struggle, but a Cast Iron Plant, ZZ Plant or Chinese Evergreen will cope well. A corner that was a dead space becomes a quiet pocket of green.

Step 5: Keep the palette calm

  • Choose pots in two or three natural tones, such as cream stoneware, terracotta and pale wood, rather than many colours.
  • Repeat one leaf shape in a few places to make the room feel connected.
  • Leave some surfaces empty. Breathing space is part of the design.

The finished room

One tall plant by the window, a few trailing plants overhead, sturdy foliage in the corners and a calm, natural palette. The room has the same footprint, but it feels bigger, softer and more alive.
