Golden Pothos care guide

Epipremnum aureum

Heart-shaped, gold-splashed leaves that trail or climb. One of the easiest plants there is.

DifficultyEasy
LightLow light, bright, indirect light
PetsToxic to pets
AttentionVery little attention

⚠️ Keep away from pets. The Golden Pothos is toxic to cats and dogs if eaten. Place it out of reach, or choose a pet-friendly plant instead.

How often to water a Golden Pothos

When the top half of the soil is dry.

How much light does a Golden Pothos need?

Low to bright, indirect light; more light means more gold.

Humidity

Average.

Feeding

Monthly in spring and summer.

Common problems

Long bare stems mean it wants more light. Trim to make it bushier.

Quick answers

Is the Golden Pothos safe for cats and dogs?

No. It is toxic to cats and dogs if eaten, so keep it out of reach. If your pet eats any plant and seems unwell, contact your vet.

Is the Golden Pothos good for beginners?

Yes, it is one of the more forgiving houseplants and copes well with the odd missed watering.

Can a Golden Pothos grow in low light?

Yes, it tolerates lower light, though it will grow more slowly.
